Curatorial text

This exhibition brings together three net art works created between 1995 and 1997. Each artist turned a basic feature of the early web into art. In Automatic Rain (1995), JODI used the blink tag and slow dial-up loading to make blue text flash down a black screen like rain. In My Boyfriend Came Back from the War (1996), Olia Lialina used frames and clickable links to divide a love story into smaller pieces. In Form Art (1997), Alexei Shulgin turned web form elements such as buttons and checkboxes into visual compositions. Although the works look different, they share the same approach, where they use the building blocks of web design as artistic materials rather than simply as tools for displaying information.

The artists were part of the 1990s net.art movement, when creators were exploring the internet as a new medium for art. Shulgin made Form Art partly in response to HTML experiments by artists like JODI. Shulgin and Lialina, both born in Moscow, are pictured together at the computer of Cine Fantom, the film club she organized. The three works show how ordinary browser features could shape an artwork’s appearance and the way people experienced it.

The works

Work 1 of 3

Automatic Rain

Artist: JODI (Joan Heemskerk and Dirk Paesmans)
Year: 1995

Blue text flashes and trickles down a black web page like rain, made with the blink tag and the slow loading of dial-up internet. The artists were inspired by the automatic lawn sprinklers they saw while living near Silicon Valley.

Work 2 of 3

My Boyfriend Came Back From the War

Artist: Olia Lialina
Year: 1996

A love story told through clickable fragments of text and image. With every click, the browser window splits into smaller frames, so the story is built from links and frames.

Work 3 of 3

Form Art

Artist: Alexei Shulgin
Year: 1997

Compositions made from ordinary web-form buttons, checkboxes and boxes, which turn the dullest part of a website into art. Shulgin also ran a competition inviting other people to make their own.
